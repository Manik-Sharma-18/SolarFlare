\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

\vspace{0.8cm}

This thesis investigates self-supervised pretraining of a deep neural encoder
on solar magnetic winding-flux cubes derived from Solar Dynamics Observatory
(SDO) Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager (HMI) observations of active regions.
The work was carried out in the Department of Physics, Indian Institute of
Technology (Banaras Hindu University), Varanasi, between August~2025 and
May~2026, as part of the Integrated Dual Degree (B.Tech.\ + M.Tech.) programme
in Engineering Physics.

\noindent\textbf{Subject.}\quad
The subject is the design and empirical validation of a Joint-Embedding
Predictive Architecture (JEPA), trained from scratch on a corpus of
twenty-one magnetic-winding-flux datacubes at 12-minute cadence, with the
ultimate goal of providing a reusable pretrained encoder for downstream solar
flare forecasting.

\noindent\textbf{Scope.}\quad
The scope is confined to pretraining and to a downstream linear winding-flux
regression probe. Full flare event forecasting on the Geostationary
Operational Environmental Satellite (GOES) X-ray catalog is identified as a
necessary follow-up but is deliberately left outside the scope of the present
thesis. Path~A, an earlier plan based on parameter-efficient fine-tuning
(LoRA) of the publicly released Surya foundation model, was abandoned on
account of an architectural incompatibility with the variable spatial extent
and 12-minute cadence of the available active-region cubes; only the
methodological lessons learned from that effort are carried forward.

\noindent\textbf{Aims.}\quad
Three concrete aims drive the work: (i) to demonstrate that the V-JEPA-2-AC
template can be reproduced at small scale on a single-channel, signed
pseudoscalar field; (ii) to characterise the sensitivity of the pretraining
objective to mask policy, mask ratio and EMA decay; and (iii) to bound the
quality of the resulting encoder by a parameter-light downstream probe on a
held-out active region.

\noindent\textbf{Philosophy of approach.}\quad
The empirical methodology favours small, controlled ablations over
single-shot large runs. Every claim about an architectural or hyperparameter
choice is supported by at least one controlled comparison in which only the
variable of interest changes. Negative and abandoned results are reported
alongside successful ones, in order to keep the chain of reasoning auditable.
All measurements are reported in SI units; all narrative is in the third
person, in accordance with the Institute's thesis guidelines.

\noindent The thesis is organised in seven chapters, four appendices, and an
extended abstract that is bound separately as required by the Institute's
thesis format.
