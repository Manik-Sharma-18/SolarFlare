\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

\section{Concept and motivation}
\label{sec:intro-concept}

Solar flares are the dominant driver of high-energy space weather.
A single $\mathrm{X}$-class flare deposits between $10^{25}$ and
$10^{26}$~$\mathrm{J}$ of energy into the corona on a timescale of
minutes, accelerating charged particles to GeV energies and emitting
a short pulse of soft and hard X-rays~\cite{aschwanden2005physics,priest2014mhd}. The terrestrial consequences
are operational: short-wave radio absorption on the dayside,
single-event upsets in low-orbit electronics, navigation outages,
and elevated radiation dose to crewed missions. The minimum useful
forecast horizon is between six and twenty-four
hours~\cite{bobra2014sharp}. The pre-flare configuration of the
photospheric magnetic field is the most reliable observable known to
contain that information.

\textbf{Thesis statement.} This work demonstrates that a
joint-embedding predictive architecture, pretrained from scratch
on twenty-one active-region cubes, yields a frozen feature
extractor whose features regress the literature-validated flare
precursor $\langle W \rangle(t)$ of
Raphaldini~et~al.~\cite{raphaldini2022winding} on five unseen
active regions to median absolute percentage error $9.9\%$,
beating lag-one persistence on four of five (Finding~F9). The
present chapter sets out the motivation; the validation is
recorded in Chapter~\ref{ch:probe}.

The present work pretrains a representation of one component of the
photospheric magnetic field --- the signed magnetic winding flux
$W(\mathbf{r}, t)$, a pseudoscalar tracer of the chirality of
emerging flux tubes~\cite{prior2020helicity,raphaldini2022winding}
--- with the explicit goal of producing a frozen feature extractor
suitable for downstream flare forecasting. No flare-event labels are
used during pretraining; the representation is acquired by
self-supervised masked prediction in embedding space.

\section{Problem statement}
\label{sec:intro-problem}

Modern flare-forecasting systems separate into two regimes. Pixel-
or image-only forecasters operate directly on
Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager (HMI)~\cite{scherrer2012hmi} or
Atmospheric Imaging Assembly (AIA) cutouts and reach a true skill
statistic (TSS) of approximately $0.7$. Multimodal hybrids that
fuse pixel features with photospheric scalar summaries (the
Space-weather HMI Active Region Patch (SHARP) parameters of
Bobra and Couvidat~\cite{bobra2014sharp}) reach
a higher TSS reported in the published flare-forecasting
literature. The gap is consistent across architectures and is
interpreted as an information ceiling on pixel-only inputs rather
than a deficiency of any particular model.

A second, separate problem concerns pretraining. The standard
video self-supervised regime~\cite{tong2022videomae,wang2023videomaev2,
he2022mae} validates on roughly three thousand videos before
linear-probe quality is judged informative. Solar physics offers
considerably less: the present corpus consists of twenty-one
active-region cubes. The constraint that defines the present work
is therefore: pretrain a self-supervised encoder for solar magnetic
data at a corpus size two orders of magnitude smaller than the
nearest published regime, without sacrificing the architectural
template that succeeded in the larger regime.

\section{Prior investigations}
\label{sec:intro-prior}

Three lines of prior work bear on the design.

\textit{Pixel-space reconstruction.} The earliest version of the
present pipeline (here designated V4) used a pixel-space forecaster
trained with a combination of $L_{1}$ and structural-similarity
losses~\cite{pathak2016contextencoders}. The model exhibited the
characteristic ``persistence collapse'' of pixel-MSE forecasters:
the output decayed to the input frame as training progressed,
because the optimal pixel-space prediction of a noisy field is
its conditional mean and the conditional mean over short horizons
is dominated by the most recent observation. The collapse is a
published failure mode of pixel-space reconstruction objectives on
high-frequency natural fields.

\textit{Foundation models for solar physics.} The NASA--IBM Surya
model~\cite{surya2025,suryabench2025} pretrains on nine years of
twelve-channel Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO) data at
$4096^{2}$ spatial resolution, using forecasting as pretext. Surya is the strongest published
pixel-only solar foundation model and reaches a flare TSS of
$0.436$. An earlier attempt within the present work (designated
Path~A) explored a Low-Rank Adaptation
(LoRA)~\cite{hu2021lora} of the public Surya weights to the active-region cube format. That path
was abandoned in May~2026 once it became clear that the
HelioSpectFormer backbone is hard-locked to $4096 \times 4096$
inputs at sixty-minute cadence with thirteen channels, none of
which the present cube format supplies. The architectural
incompatibility is recorded as Finding~F10 and forms the
motivation for Path~B (Finding~F10 in
Section~\ref{sec:exp-findings}).

\textit{Joint-embedding predictive architectures.} The V-JEPA
family~\cite{assran2024vjepa,vjepa2_2025,vjepa21_2026,assran2023ijepa}
demonstrates that prediction in embedding space, with an
exponential-moving-average (EMA)-updated target encoder and no
contrastive negatives, scales to
spatiotemporal video corpora and outperforms pixel reconstruction
on downstream probes. The V-JEPA-2-AC variant adds a block-causal
predictor and is the architectural template adapted in
Chapter~\ref{ch:architecture}.

\section{Scope and objectives}
\label{sec:intro-scope}

This thesis develops Path~B: a joint-embedding predictive
architecture pretrained from scratch on twenty-one active-region
cubes of magnetic winding flux. Path~A is treated as historical
context (\S\ref{sec:intro-prior}); no LoRA adaptation of Surya is
attempted in the present work. The primary downstream task is the
spatial-mean winding-flux regression probe of
Chapter~\ref{ch:probe}; a secondary binary flare classifier is
reported in \S\ref{sec:probe-flare} on labels merged from the
Geostationary Operational Environmental Satellite (GOES) X-ray
event catalogue for the nineteen labelled cubes of the corpus.

The explicit objectives are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Validate the V-JEPA-2-AC template at a small scale (twenty-one
cubes, twelve-minute cadence, single-channel signed pseudoscalar
field with variable spatial extent).
\item Identify the operating point in the four-dimensional design
space spanned by mask-policy mixture, mask ratio, target-encoder
decay~$\tau$, and training-step budget.
\item Probe the resulting frozen features for the spatial-mean
winding flux $\langle W \rangle(t)$, the literature-supported
flare precursor of Raphaldini et al.~\cite{raphaldini2022winding},
and quantify the gap between pretraining-only performance and
operational flare-forecast skill.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro-contrib}

The contributions of the present work, by chapter, are as follows.

\begin{itemize}
\item Chapter~\ref{ch:data}: a preprocessing pipeline for variable-
shape signed pseudoscalar magnetic-field data, including a
chirality-respecting $D_{4}$ augmentation scheme, a winding-flux
guard of $|W| \le 10^{8}$, and a cube-level
(HMI Active Region Patch (HARP) identity) holdout convention.

\item Chapter~\ref{ch:architecture}: an adaptation of the
V-JEPA-2-AC template to signed scalar fields of variable extent,
with a five-family mask catalogue, a two-stage curriculum, and a
loss-mask intersection $M_{\text{loss}} = M_{\text{mask}} \land
M_{\text{valid}} \land M_{\text{pad}}$ that excludes padding,
sparse-data, and unmasked-target tokens by construction.

\item Chapter~\ref{ch:experiments}: a four-family ablation
covering mask-policy mixture (E12--E15), EMA decay
(E17--E20), mask ratio (E25--E28), and the scaling step
to the thesis-scale run (E30). The uniform mask mixture is shown
to dominate every tube-heavy mixture tested (Finding~F11) and the
EMA-decay response is monotone in the tested range
(\S\ref{sec:exp-ema}).

\item Chapter~\ref{ch:probe}: a frozen-feature wind-flux probe on
the E30~v2 thesis-scale checkpoint, attaining $R^{2} = 0.73$
(multilayer perceptron (MLP), $r = 0.87$, median absolute
percentage error (medAPE) of $7.3\%$) on the encoder-validation
cubes and a medAPE of $9.9\%$ on five novel cubes after a one-pair
per-cube log-affine recalibration (Finding~F9, confirmed), beating
the lag-one persistence baseline on four of five novel cubes; and
a secondary frozen-feature flare classifier that delivers
area-under-the-ROC-curve (AUC) $= 1.000$ and TSS $= 1.000$ on
harp\_49 at the
C$+$/12~h--MLP configuration, exceeding the persistence floor of
$0.975$ on the densest mixed-label evaluation cube
(Finding~F13).

\item Chapter~\ref{ch:summary}: a roadmap toward operational
forecasting, identifying the four components --- larger corpus,
downstream head, persistence-anchored skill metrics, and
multimodal SHARP fusion --- that separate the present pretraining
result from a deployable forecaster.
\end{itemize}

\section{Organisation of the thesis}
\label{sec:intro-org}

Chapter~\ref{ch:background} reviews the physics of magnetic
winding flux, the joint-embedding predictive doctrine, and the
prior solar foundation-model literature. Chapter~\ref{ch:data}
specifies the preprocessing pipeline. Chapter~\ref{ch:architecture}
specifies the model. Chapter~\ref{ch:experiments} reports the
ablations and the thesis-scale run. Chapter~\ref{ch:probe} reports
the downstream probe and the flare-forecasting gap.
Chapter~\ref{ch:summary} summarises the present contributions and
proposes future work. Hyperparameter tables, the mask catalogue,
the winding-flux clipping audit, and per-run convergence curves
are recorded in Appendices~\ref{app:hyperparams}--\ref{app:curves}.
