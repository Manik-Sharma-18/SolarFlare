\chapter{Technical Architecture}
\label{ch:arch}

This chapter specifies the complete forecasting system: the winding-flux data
and how it is represented (\S\ref{sec:data}), the Convolutional LSTM cell and the
encoder--forecaster assembled from it (\S\ref{sec:convlstm}--\S\ref{sec:model}),
and the training procedure (\S\ref{sec:training}). Every design choice that
affects the reported results---in particular the spatial low-pass and the choice
of the \emph{accumulated} winding field---is stated explicitly so that the
outcome in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} can be read without hidden assumptions.

\section{Data and representation}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Winding-flux datacubes.}
The input data are per-active-region datacubes derived from SDO/HMI
observations. \emph{Winding flux} measures the rate at which magnetic
\emph{winding}---a signed, topological measure of how an active region's field
lines wrap around one another \citep{mactaggart2021}---is carried through the
photosphere. It is obtained from the time sequence of photospheric vector
magnetograms by accumulating, across the region, the winding contributed by the
moving and emerging magnetic flux between successive frames. The scheme used here
follows this winding-flux accounting in spirit---close to recent tools of the
same kind, though not identical to any single published implementation---and its
output is a \emph{rate}: a per-frame quantity, not an accumulated one. Each cube
stores a single channel $\mathrm{wind}[H,W,T]$---this winding-flux \emph{rate}
density on an $H\times W$ spatial grid at $T$ successive times---together with a
vector of acquisition timestamps at a nominal $12$-minute cadence. That the
stored field is a rate, and not the accumulated winding itself, is the fact that
determines the forecasting target below. A small number of frames are missing; these are flagged by a sentinel
timestamp of zero and are removed before any windowing, so that the model only
ever sees physically contiguous sequences. The corpus used here comprises the
available HARP cubes; one cube exhibiting pathological, noise-dominated values
is excluded from every experiment.

\paragraph{The active-region corpus.}
The dataset comprises $27$ active regions and $13{,}472$ valid frames after
sentinel removal, a median of $421$ frames per region
(Figure~\ref{fig:dataset}); the regions vary widely in size---from a few hundred
to over a thousand pixels on a side---and in the morphology of their winding
field (Figure~\ref{fig:gallery}). Each map is signed and bipolar, with opposed
regions of positive and negative winding meeting along the internal boundaries of
the active region. This diversity is the reason the evaluation is
leave-one-active-region-out (\S\ref{sec:training}): the model must generalise
across regions of different scale and structure, not merely interpolate within
one. Table~\ref{tab:dataset} lists every region with its frame count and grid
size. The corpus also carries the characteristic imprint of how the data are
measured: regions observed near the solar limb suffer the \emph{limb effect},
where line-of-sight foreshortening degrades the vector-field inversion and leaves
part of the patch with unreliable, low-signal winding (visible in HARP~892 in
Figure~\ref{fig:gallery}). Such near-limb passages are a known limitation of
photospheric magnetography and bound the usable field of view for any method
built on these maps. Near-limb frames are retained rather than masked: their
degraded winding enters the pooled training set as an additional source of noise,
and robustness to it is part of what the leave-one-region-out evaluation
(\S\ref{sec:training}) measures.

\paragraph{Cadence and gaps.}
The nominal cadence is $12$~minutes, and the overwhelming majority of steps
respect it exactly (Figure~\ref{fig:cadence}). A small fraction---under one per
cent---are longer, appearing at integer multiples of the cadence where one or
more acquisitions are missing. These gaps are why windowing must be
\emph{gap-aware}: a window whose ten input and four target frames are not all
consecutive in real time would splice across a temporal discontinuity and
present the model with a jump that never occurs in deployment. Every training and
evaluation window used in this thesis is gap-free by construction.

\paragraph{The signed field and its dynamic range.}
Winding-flux density is a \emph{signed} pseudoscalar with a very large dynamic
range. Its distribution is sharply peaked at zero but heavy-tailed: the bulk of
pixels lie near the origin while a few reach magnitudes of order $10^{7}$, several
decades above the typical value (Figure~\ref{fig:norm}, left). Training a network
directly on such values is unstable---the rare extremes dominate the loss and the
gradients. The field is therefore passed through a sign-preserving \emph{inverse
hyperbolic sine} (asinh) transform,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{w} \;=\; \operatorname{asinh}\!\left(\frac{w}{s}\right),
  \qquad s = 10^{3},
  \label{eq:asinh}
\end{equation}
which is linear for $|w|\ll s$ and logarithmic for $|w|\gg s$
(Figure~\ref{fig:norm}, centre). This compresses the extreme tail while
preserving sign and small-signal structure, turning the raw heavy-tailed
distribution into the well-behaved, roughly symmetric one of
Figure~\ref{fig:norm} (right); the softening constant $s=10^{3}$ was fixed by
prior probing of the field's amplitude distribution. The transformed field is
then rescaled by a robust ($99.5$th percentile) amplitude and clipped to
$[-1,1]$ per cube, giving a normalised, zero-centred representation comparable
across active regions. The clip is a mild guardrail, not a lossy compression: on
the accumulated total it saturates only the top $0.5\%$ of pixels and discards
under $0.1\%$ of the field's summed amplitude---the asinh transform has already
compressed the extremes, so the most extreme pixel sits barely above the clip
($\sim\!1.15\times$ the threshold). Its real purpose is to contain the rare
pathological cube whose outliers would otherwise dominate the robust scale and
the loss. A control run confirms this is not a sensitive choice: widening the
limit to $[-1.5,1.5]$ leaves the held-out skill unchanged (within $0.001$ under
an identical training configuration), as Table~\ref{tab:clip} records.

\paragraph{The accumulated total winding.}
The quantity this thesis forecasts is the \emph{accumulated total} winding: the
net magnetic winding injected into an active region up to time $t$, obtained by
integrating the per-frame winding-flux rate over time,
\begin{equation}
  W[t] \;=\; \sum_{\tau \le t} \mathrm{wind}[\tau].
  \label{eq:cumsum}
\end{equation}
This is the physically meaningful measure of an active region's accumulated
non-potential topology, and it is the natural forecasting target: it is smooth
and strongly persistent from frame to frame, with a lag-of-one spatial
correlation of about $0.98$ that decays only gradually over hours
(Figure~\ref{fig:autocorr}). Its gradual build-up is exactly what a
spatiotemporal model can learn to extrapolate. Unless stated otherwise, the
model is trained and evaluated on the accumulated total $W$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cumsum}, consistent with the total winding used throughout this
project.

\paragraph{The spatial mean discards the signal.}
The premise of this thesis is that reducing a winding map to a single number
destroys the very structure that matters, and the data show it directly.
Figure~\ref{fig:spmean_rate} plots, for nine representative regions, the
\emph{spatial mean} of the winding flux---the scalar the literature forecasts.
It jitters about zero: the map is bipolar, and its positive and negative winding
lobes very nearly cancel when averaged. Its magnitude, a few times
$10^{2}$--$10^{3}$, sits four to five orders of magnitude below the per-pixel
amplitude ($\sim\!10^{7}$)---almost the entire field has been averaged away.
Accumulating that scalar in time (Figure~\ref{fig:spmean_total}) gives a smooth
one-dimensional drift: forecastable, but carrying none of the spatial
organisation that distinguishes one configuration from another. Both figures are
gap-aware---shaded bands mark acquisition gaps, across which the series is broken
rather than interpolated. Together they are the empirical case for the central
choice of this work: to keep the map rather than collapse it.

\paragraph{Spatial low-pass and downsampling.}
Before entering the network each frame is area-downsampled to the fixed
$88\times132$ grid and convolved with a separable Gaussian kernel of width
$\sigma=1.5$~px,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{W}_{\text{lp}} = G_\sigma * \tilde{W},
  \qquad G_\sigma(r)\propto \exp\!\big(-r^2/2\sigma^2\big),
  \label{eq:lowpass}
\end{equation}
applied as two one-dimensional passes (along rows, then columns). This is a
deliberate, disclosed choice, and the data motivate it directly. The winding
field carries appreciable power at every spatial scale, right down to the pixel:
its radially-averaged power spectrum falls off only slowly and never flattens into
a noise floor (Figure~\ref{fig:psd}). Here the \emph{spatial wavenumber}
$k=2\pi/\lambda$ measures inverse spatial scale---small $k$ is the large-scale
envelope, large $k$ the pixel-scale detail---and the radially-averaged power
spectrum is the squared Fourier amplitude of the field, averaged over all
directions at fixed $|\mathbf{k}|=k$,
\begin{equation}
  P(k) = \big\langle\, \lvert\hat{\tilde{W}}(\mathbf{k})\rvert^2 \,\big\rangle_{\lvert\mathbf{k}\rvert=k},
  \qquad
  \hat{\tilde{W}}(\mathbf{k}) = \int \tilde{W}(\mathbf{x})\,e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,\mathrm{d}^2x .
  \label{eq:psd}
\end{equation} That fine-scale content is not, however, useful for
forecasting---the same small scales that the spectrum shows to be energetic are
the ones whose frame-to-frame evolution is incoherent
(Figure~\ref{fig:autocorr}), so they cannot be predicted. The low-pass discards
this energetic-but-unpredictable detail in favour of the large-scale envelope,
which evolves coherently, letting the model concentrate its capacity on the part
of the field that can actually be forecast. The cost---loss of sub-envelope
structure---is real and is acknowledged wherever results are reported.

to the gap-aware training windows.

\paragraph{The pipeline end to end.}
For full transparency, the complete transformation from the raw datacube to a
normalised model input is the single composition
\begin{equation}
  x_t \;=\; \operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}\!\left(
    \frac{1}{q}\; G_\sigma * \operatorname{asinh}\!\left[
      \frac{1}{s}\,\mathcal{D}\!\sum_{\tau\le t}\mathrm{wind}[\tau]
    \right]\right),
  \label{eq:pipeline}
\end{equation}
read from the inside out: accumulate the winding-flux \emph{rate} in time to the
total (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cumsum}); area-downsample with the operator $\mathcal{D}$ to
the $88\times132$ grid; asinh-compress with softening $s=10^3$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:asinh}); low-pass with $G_\sigma$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:lowpass}); divide
by the per-cube robust amplitude $q$ (the $99.5$th percentile) and clip to
$[-1,1]$. The normalised sequence is then cut into gap-aware windows of
$T_{\text{in}}+T_{\text{out}}=14$ consecutive frames. \emph{Within} the model
(\S\ref{sec:model}) the encoder--forecaster $f_\theta$ maps the first ten frames
to a four-frame forecast. \emph{After} the model, nothing inverts the
normalisation: prediction, ground truth, the persistence baseline, and every
reported error all live in these same normalised units, so the skill of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:skill} is a strictly like-for-like comparison. Equation
\eqref{eq:pipeline}, the windowing, $f_\theta$, and this evaluation convention are
the entire path from a photospheric observation to a reported number.

\section{The Convolutional LSTM cell}
\label{sec:convlstm}

The temporal backbone of the model is the Convolutional LSTM (ConvLSTM) of
\citet{shi2015}, which adapts the Long Short-Term Memory unit of
\citet{hochreiter1997} to spatial data. A standard LSTM maintains a memory cell
gated by learned nonlinearities but treats its input as a flat vector, destroying
spatial structure. The ConvLSTM keeps the same gating logic but replaces every
matrix multiplication with a two-dimensional convolution, so that the hidden
state $h$ and cell state $c$ are \emph{feature maps} rather than vectors, and
spatial locality is preserved throughout the recurrence.

At each timestep the current input frame $x_t$ and the previous hidden state
$h_{t-1}$ (both feature maps) are concatenated along the channel axis and passed
through a single convolution whose output is split into four gate maps---input
$i$, forget $f$, candidate $g$ and output $o$:
\begin{align}
  i_t &= \sigma\!\big(W_i * [\,x_t, h_{t-1}\,] + b_i\big), &
  f_t &= \sigma\!\big(W_f * [\,x_t, h_{t-1}\,] + b_f\big), \nonumber\\
  g_t &= \tanh\!\big(W_g * [\,x_t, h_{t-1}\,] + b_g\big), &
  o_t &= \sigma\!\big(W_o * [\,x_t, h_{t-1}\,] + b_o\big),
  \label{eq:gates}
\end{align}
where $*$ denotes convolution and $\sigma$ the logistic sigmoid. The cell and
hidden states update as
\begin{equation}
  c_t = f_t \odot c_{t-1} + i_t \odot g_t,
  \qquad
  h_t = o_t \odot \tanh(c_t),
  \label{eq:cell}
\end{equation}
with $\odot$ the elementwise product. Computing all four gates with one
convolution is an efficiency choice; using ``same'' padding keeps the spatial
resolution fixed through the recurrence. Two implementation details matter for
stable training: the forget-gate bias $b_f$ is initialised to $+1$, so that the
cell defaults to \emph{remembering} its state early in training and gradients
propagate cleanly through time; and normalisation between stacked layers uses
group normalisation, which carries no running statistics and is therefore safe
to reuse across the unrolled decoding loop. Figure~\ref{fig:cell} traces the
internal dataflow of a single cell.

\section{Encoder--forecaster model}
\label{sec:model}

The full model is an \emph{encoder--forecaster}: a stack of ConvLSTM layers first
reads the observed history, and a second stack then generates the future frames
autoregressively. Given an input clip of $T_{\text{in}}=10$ frames, the encoder
applies Eq.~\eqref{eq:gates}--\eqref{eq:cell} layer by layer and frame by frame,
accumulating the sequence into the hidden and cell states of every layer. No
output is produced during encoding; its sole purpose is to summarise the history
into the recurrent state. Concretely, for $L$ stacked layers the encoder updates,
at each frame $t=1,\dots,T_{\text{in}}$ and layer $\ell=1,\dots,L$,
\begin{equation}
  (h^{\ell}_t, c^{\ell}_t) = \operatorname{ConvLSTM}^{\ell}\!\big(z^{\ell-1}_t,\,
    h^{\ell}_{t-1},\, c^{\ell}_{t-1}\big),
  \qquad z^{0}_t = x_t,\quad z^{\ell}_t = h^{\ell}_t,
  \label{eq:encoder}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{ConvLSTM}^{\ell}$ is one application of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:gates}--\eqref{eq:cell} and $z^{\ell}_t$ is the map handed up to the
next layer. After the final input frame the states
$\{(h^{\ell}_{T_{\text{in}}}, c^{\ell}_{T_{\text{in}}})\}_{\ell=1}^{L}$ carry the
entire observed history.

Decoding then rolls the state forward for $T_{\text{out}}=4$ steps. At each step
the decoder stack advances the recurrent state and a $1\times1$ convolution maps
the top hidden map to a single-channel prediction. Crucially the model predicts a
\emph{residual}: the output is the previous frame plus a learned increment read
off the top hidden map by a $1\times1$ convolution $W_{\text{out}}$,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}_{t+1} = x_t + \Delta_\theta(h_t),
  \qquad \Delta_\theta(h_t) = W_{\text{out}} * h^{L}_t,
  \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
so that a do-nothing network falls back to \emph{persistence}---copying the last
frame---rather than collapsing to zero. This is the appropriate inductive bias
for a strongly persistent field and, as Chapter~\ref{ch:results} makes clear, it
also sets the correct baseline against which any genuine skill must be measured.
The predicted frame is fed back as the next decoder input, so errors compound
along the roll-out exactly as they would at deployment. Figure~\ref{fig:model}
shows the encoder, the state hand-off, and the autoregressive decoder together.

\input{figures/fig_encoder_forecaster}

During training the fed-back frame may instead be the ground-truth frame with a
tunable probability---\emph{teacher forcing}---which stabilises early optimisation
by anchoring the roll-out to real data; at evaluation teacher forcing is switched
off and the model runs purely on its own predictions.

\paragraph{Forward propagation, with shapes.}
Figure~\ref{fig:forward} traces one forward pass from end to end, carrying the
tensor shape $(C,T,H,W)$ at every stage (the batch dimension is suppressed). A
normalised input window is a tensor $x\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times10\times88\times132}$.
The encoder consumes it one frame at a time: each frame
$x_t\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times88\times132}$ is passed up the three-layer stack, every
layer applying its fused gate convolution to the channel-concatenation
$[x_t,h^{\ell}_{t-1}]$ and updating a hidden and cell map
$h^{\ell},c^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{96\times88\times132}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:encoder}).
The first layer's convolution has weight
$W\in\mathbb{R}^{384\times97\times3\times3}$---its $4\times96$ output channels are
the four gates, from the $1+96$ input channels---and the two deeper layers
$W\in\mathbb{R}^{384\times192\times3\times3}$. After the tenth frame the encoder's
final states $\{(h^{\ell},c^{\ell})\}_{\ell=1}^{3}$ summarise the history and are
handed to a \emph{separate} three-layer decoder stack as its initial states. The
decoder then rolls forward four steps: at each step it advances the states, a
$1\times1$ convolution $W_{\text{out}}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times96\times1\times1}$
reads an increment $\Delta=W_{\text{out}}*h^{L}$ off the top hidden map, and the
residual $\hat{x}_{t+1}=x_t+\Delta$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual}) is both an output
frame and the next step's input. The four increments stack into the forecast
$\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times4\times88\times132}$. Nothing after the network
inverts the normalisation, so these four maps are scored against the truth and
against persistence in identical units. Encoder and decoder are distinct stacks,
together $3.33$~million parameters.

\section{Training procedure}
\label{sec:training}

The model is optimised with Adam and a per-frame regression loss (a smoothed
$L_1$ / Huber criterion) between the predicted and true future frames, summed
over the $T_{\text{out}}$ horizon. Gradients are clipped in norm to curb the
occasional large update produced by rare high-amplitude frames, and the learning
rate follows a cosine decay across the training budget. Table~\ref{tab:hparams}
collects the complete data, model, and training configuration in one place.

\input{figures/tab_hparams}

Evaluation follows a strict \emph{leave-one-active-region-out} protocol. Because
successive $12$-minute frames within a cube are highly autocorrelated, any split
that mixes frames from the same active region between training and test leaks
information and inflates the apparent score. The model is therefore trained on
the pooled windows of all active regions except one, and evaluated only on the
held-out region it has never seen. All figures and metrics in
Chapter~\ref{ch:results} are reported on such held-out regions, together with the
persistence baseline of Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual}, so that model skill is always
stated relative to the trivial forecast.
