# Q & A — for physicists who know the physics, not the ML

Anticipated questions from an examiner fluent in solar physics but not deep
learning / data preprocessing. Answers in plain terms, with the honest caveat.

### Q1. What is a ConvLSTM actually doing?
An LSTM is a recurrence with a memory cell and learned "gates" that decide what to
keep, forget, and output each step — it models sequences. A ConvLSTM replaces the
internal matrix multiplies with **convolutions**, so its memory is a *stack of
feature maps* rather than a flat vector, and spatial locality is preserved. So it
carries a spatial memory of the winding field forward in time. (Ch2 §2.2.)

### Q2. Why the asinh transform — isn't that distorting the physics?
The field spans decades: most pixels near zero, a few near 10⁷. A network trained
on raw values is dominated by the rare extremes. `asinh(w/s)` is **linear for
small |w|, logarithmic for large** — it is sign-preserving and invertible, so no
information is destroyed, only the *scale* is compressed. It is a monotonic
re-encoding, not a physical claim. (Ch2 §2.1, Eq. 2.1.)

### Q3. You low-pass the maps — aren't you throwing away the PIL structure you say matters? (the killer)
Partly, deliberately. Two facts: (i) the fine scales carry energy (PSD never
flattens) but (ii) they are *temporally incoherent* — they decorrelate frame to
frame (autocorrelation), so they **cannot be forecast** whatever we do. The
low-pass spends model capacity on the large-scale structure that *can* be
predicted. The cost — losing sub-envelope detail — is stated wherever results are
reported. A PIL-resolved model is future work.

### Q4. What does "skill over persistence" mean, and why not accuracy or correlation?
Persistence = "assume the next frames equal the last observed one." For a
slowly-drifting field that is already a strong forecast, so raw error looks great
for *any* method. Skill = 1 − MAE_model/MAE_persistence isolates what the model
adds *beyond* copying. Correlation is scale-free and inflated by autocorrelation;
it would flatter the model. MAE-skill is the honest, interpretable choice.

### Q5. You normalise each region separately — can the model still compare regions?
Each cube is scaled by its own robust amplitude, so absolute winding magnitude is
not comparable across regions — by design. The model learns *relative spatial
patterns and their evolution*, which is what transfers. Absolute-scale forecasting
would need a shared normalisation; that is a limitation, stated as such.

### Q6. Teacher forcing — are you feeding the network the answer during training?
During training only, with a set probability, the true frame is fed back instead of
the model's own prediction, to stabilise early learning. At **evaluation it is
switched off** — the model runs purely on its own outputs, so the reported numbers
have no leakage. (Ch2 §2.4.)

### Q7. Why forecast 48 minutes when flares are hours away?
This thesis forecasts the *field*, not the flare, as a proof that the
spatiotemporal structure is learnable; 48 min is four autoregressive steps at
12-min cadence. It is not a flare lead-time claim. The flare-relevant horizons
(6–24 h) belong to the downstream classification task. *Be explicit — this is a
scope question, not a defect.*

### Q8. Isn't the accumulated total a random walk, hence trivially predictable?
Its persistence is partly integrative, yes — which is exactly why every number is
quoted against persistence. The +8.6% (CI [+6.8,+10.3]) is the part persistence
does *not* explain, and it grows with lead time, which a pure random walk would not
produce. (Fig 3.3.)

### Q9. How do you know it isn't just overfitting / memorising the training regions?
The test region is **entirely held out** — never in training. Within-region random
splits would leak (12-min frames are autocorrelated); leave-one-AR-out prevents
that. The train loss saturates while held-out error only dips slightly — we report
the honest generalisation gap, not the training fit. (Fig 3.1.)

### Q10. What does MAE = 0.0105 mean physically?
It is in the normalised units ([−1,1] after asinh + robust scaling), so ≈ 0.5% of
full scale. It is a *relative* error on the compressed field, not a physical flux;
it is meaningful only against the persistence baseline in the same units.

### Q11. Why ConvLSTM and not a plain CNN, or a transformer?
A CNN alone has no temporal memory; a transformer needs far more data and is
memory-heavy for full-resolution maps. ConvLSTM matches the data — spatial
convolution + temporal recurrence — at 3.3 M parameters, trainable on one GPU.
Architecture is not the bottleneck here; the persistence ceiling is.

### Q12. How much resolution is 88×132, physically?
It is a downsample of native patches (hundreds to >1000 px per side). It preserves
the large-scale winding envelope (lobes, boundaries) while discarding pixel-scale
detail; the exact Mm/pixel varies by region since patches differ in size.
